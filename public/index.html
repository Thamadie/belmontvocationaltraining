What is Ausbildung?

Learn a profession — and get paid from day one

Ausbildung is Germany’s dual vocational training. You spend part of the week at a vocational school and the rest working at a training company, which pays you a monthly salary.

  • For students who have not passed A/L — O/L is enough for most programmes
  • 3-year programme in Nursing, Hotel & Hospitality, IT, Automobile or Mechatronics
  • German B1 / B2 language level, taught to you before you leave
  • After training: a full-time professional career in Germany

Questions

Frequently asked

I haven’t passed A/L. Can I still go to Germany?

Yes. Students who have not passed A/L may have opportunities through a 3-year vocational training programme (Ausbildung) in Nursing, Hotel & Hospitality, IT, Automobile or Mechatronics.

How much does the programme cost?

The programme fee is LKR 1,000,000: an initial payment of LKR 300,000, and the balance after you receive your offer, together with the blocked account. German courses are separate: A1 & A2 LKR 50,000, B1 LKR 35,000 and the B1 exam LKR 36,000.

Do I need a blocked account?

Usually yes — about €12,000, from which you can withdraw around €992 a month after arriving. After 12 months you can access the remaining balance. If you have a sponsor in Germany who meets the conditions, you may not need one.

Can I work while I’m in Germany?

Non-EU students may work up to 140 full days or 280 half-days a year, at around €13.90 an hour — roughly €12,000–€15,000 a year depending on your hours. Ausbildung trainees also receive a monthly training salary of around €1,000.

Can I bring my family?

Not immediately on a student visa. After completing your studies and getting a full-time job, you may be able to bring family members through the family reunion visa, subject to the requirements.

What happens after my training or degree?

After an Ausbildung you can start a full-time career in Germany. After a degree you may be eligible for an 18-month post-study residence permit to look for work.

Can I get permanent residence?

There may be opportunities for permanent residence and eventually citizenship. After holding a residence title continuously for five years, with German B1 and two years of full-time work, you may apply for an open-ended residence permit if the conditions are met.
